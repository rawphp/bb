Before you start, make sure you have a bathroom scale, access to a gym and a gram food scale. Each of the three sharpens the accuracy of your plan.

## Weighing your food

Every meal target is written in grams. Weighing your food on a scale with 1 g steps takes the guesswork out of landing on those numbers. Measuring cups and spoons drift, so the same scoop holds more on some days than others.

## Weighing yourself

Your plan is guided by where your weight is heading over time. Any scale you trust will do, provided you can get on it every few days.

- Two weigh-in days a week is the floor. Four or more is better.
- Weigh yourself right after waking and using the bathroom, wearing as little as possible.
- Day-to-day numbers jump up and down, so no single one means much. Watch the direction instead.
- Smart scales often report body fat. Ignore that figure.

## A place to lift

A gym works, and so does a home setup that lets you hit every major muscle group. Owning only dumbbells, a bench, a pull-up bar and a barbell loaded with plates will still get you a long way.

The app never asks what equipment you have. If your space is limited, start from a template and replace whatever exercises your gear can't handle.

## Helpful extras

- Letting the app into Apple Health means it can pull in your weight and step count. Turn this on or off in Settings.
- Bring your phone when you train, and record each set as you finish it.
