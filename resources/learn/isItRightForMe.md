Bodybuilder is for people who lift and want training and eating run from one plan. A handful of health situations need a doctor's go-ahead first.

## You'll likely get on well with it if

- You can fit 2 to 4 lifting days into every week, week after week. A schedule you keep is worth more than a 5 or 6 day one you skip.
- You have a clear aim: losing fat, building muscle or staying put.
- Regular weigh-ins and meal check-ins don't bother you.
- You'd rather the app handle the adjusting than do the math yourself.

## Check with a doctor before starting if

Before any calorie targets appear, you answer a short health questionnaire. If one of these fits you, get medical clearance before you diet.

- You're younger than 18 or older than 65.
- A baby is on the way, you're breastfeeding, or you're trying to conceive.
- You have diabetes, PCOS, an autoimmune disease, gallbladder disease, or a heart or thyroid condition.
- Within the last 5 years, you had weight-loss surgery.
- You use hormone therapy.
- You've had an eating disorder at any point.

Answer yes to any of these and the app switches calorie targets off. The one exception is trying to conceive, which blocks only fat loss. Training features and weigh-ins keep working.

## GLP-1 medication

Semaglutide and tirzepatide are examples. Mention it when the app asks. Each meal then lists protein first. Two straight weeks of eating far under target prompt a muscle-loss warning in your weekly review. For medication and diet decisions, follow your doctor.

## Not covered

Medical diets are unsupported. People with a metabolic condition should check with a physician before using the nutrition features.
