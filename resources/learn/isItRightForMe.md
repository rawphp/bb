Bodybuilder is built for people who lift and want one plan to cover both their workouts and their food. If certain health conditions apply to you, a doctor needs to sign off before you diet.

## Who tends to get the most out of it

- You train with weights.
- Your schedule allows 2–4 sessions a week on a fixed pattern. Showing up for that reliably does more than planning 5–6 days and missing some.
- You know what you're after, whether that's dropping fat, adding muscle or staying where you are.
- Stepping on the scale often and checking in meals doesn't bother you.
- You'd like the app to handle the adjustments so you aren't running numbers yourself.

## Health screening comes first

No calorie target appears until you've gone through a brief set of health questions. Get medical clearance from your doctor before you begin any diet when one or more of these is true:

- You're younger than 18, or older than 65.
- Your baby is on the way, or you're breastfeeding.
- You're trying to conceive, which blocks only fat loss.
- You live with diabetes, PCOS, or an autoimmune, gallbladder, heart or thyroid condition.
- It has been under 5 years since your weight-loss surgery.
- You're taking hormone therapy.
- At any point in your life, you've had an eating disorder.

A yes on the conception question removes fat loss and nothing else. A yes anywhere else turns off every calorie target. Training features and weigh-ins carry on regardless.

## On a GLP-1 drug

Semaglutide and tirzepatide are two examples. Let the app know when the question comes up, and stick with what your doctor tells you about the drug and about eating. From then on, each meal lists protein at the top. Eat far below target for two straight weeks and the weekly review cautions that muscle may be slipping away.

## Outside what the app supports

Medical diets are unsupported. Anyone with a metabolic condition should speak with a physician before turning to the nutrition side of the app.
